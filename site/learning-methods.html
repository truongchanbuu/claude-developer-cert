Study with a purpose

Understand. Recall. Compare. Return.

The companion turns each original exercise into a small learning cycle. Research informs the design; the website itself has not been tested for certification outcomes.

1. Use an example when a topic is new

Open the different worked example. Explain why its key step fits the situation, then close it and attempt the related exercise. You can use a hint when you need support.

Self-explanation research examined learners connecting worked steps with principles. A later experiment also found benefits from prompting explanations during reading. Applying these findings to developer exercises is a design inference.

Chi et al., 1989: self-explanations and worked examples (new tab) · Chi et al., 1994: prompting self-explanation (PDF, new tab)

2. Try recalling before opening the answer

Close resources and previous notes. State your answer, the reason it works, and why a tempting alternative would fail. You can speak or write; saving a note is optional.

In experiments with prose, retrieval practice supported delayed retention better than additional reading, even though rereading performed better on an immediate test. Familiarity while reading is not the same as being able to recall later.

Roediger and Karpicke, 2006: retrieval practice (new tab)

3. Compare and correct your reasoning

Open the explained solution after your attempt. Identify one thing you got right and one correction, including why the correction matters. Use the note editor if you want to keep that insight.

Choose Needed support, Partly recalled, or Recalled independently after comparing. This is your own report, not automatic grading or a measure of mastery. Visiting a solution never marks the activity complete.

4. Return on another day

Recording recall puts the next date in your review plan: one day after needing support, three days after partial recall, or seven days after independent recall. Due exercises appear on the Review page. There are no reminders or notifications outside the site.

Spacing research found that useful gaps depend on the desired retention period. Our 1/3/7-day choices are simple planning defaults, not intervals proven optimal for you or this exam. Review earlier if you need to, and try recalling again before revealing notes.

Cepeda et al., 2008: spacing and retention (new tab)

These studies used prose, facts, science passages, or mechanics problems. They do not establish that this companion improves Claude certification scores. Use the methods alongside official resources and actual development practice.
